Executive Search for Nonprofits: A Guide for Boards & Hiring Teams

The Discovery Lens

Recruiting a new CEO, Executive Director, or philanthropy leader is one of the most significant decisions hiring committees and boards will make. You’re not just filling a vacancy, you’re choosing a leader who will advance your mission, strengthen your culture, build meaningful relationships with your community and supporters, and guide your organization through its next chapter.

The social profit sector is evolving rapidly. Organizations are navigating increased complexity, shifting funding landscapes, growing expectations, and changing workforce dynamics. In this environment, executive search requires a thoughtful, strategic approach that reflects both the realities of leadership today and the unique needs of social profit organizations.

In our recent webinar, Christoph Clodius, Vice President at The Discovery Group shared practical guidance for boards and hiring committees preparing for their next leadership transition. Whether you’re beginning to think about succession planning or actively recruiting your next executive leader, these are some of the key insights from the discussion.

Phase 1: Preparing to Hire — Set the Foundation for Success

Successful executive searches begin long before the role is posted. The first step is getting clear on what success looks like, why the role exists, and how it will contribute to your organization’s goals, rather than simply creating a list of qualifications.

Before launching a search, align decision-makers, distinguish true must-haves from nice-to-haves, set realistic expectations around compensation and timelines, and address any internal barriers that could prevent a new hire from succeeding. Just as importantly, identify what makes your organization stand out to top candidates.

The stronger your preparation, the stronger your search, and the better positioned you’ll be to hire someone who can deliver meaningful results.

Phase 2: Putting Your Role in the Public – Build a Position Profile That Inspires

A position profile is more than a job description. It’s your opportunity to tell your organization’s story. Lead with your mission and the difference the successful candidate will make, helping prospective leaders connect with the purpose behind the role.

Write for the candidate, not just your HR requirements. Use clear, inclusive language, focus on the opportunity rather than a lengthy list of qualifications, and avoid hiring for “fit.” Instead, prioritize mission alignment, competencies, and the diverse perspectives that strengthen organizations.

Finally, be transparent. Clearly communicate salary, benefits, expectations, and the unique aspects of your workplace so candidates understand both the opportunity and the challenges ahead. The strongest position profiles inspire the right people to apply.

Phase 3: Sourcing – Be Strategic About Sourcing Candidates

Finding exceptional leaders requires more than posting a job and waiting for applications. The strongest searches take a proactive approach, leveraging networks, referrals, and targeted outreach to connect with candidates who may not be actively looking.

Expand your reach by engaging your professional community, sharing opportunities through trusted connections, and intentionally sourcing from diverse networks to build a broader, more representative candidate pool. Be responsive to questions, personalize your outreach, and move quickly. Top candidates are often considering multiple opportunities at once.

Finally, pay attention to what works. Tracking where candidates come from, and which messages resonate will help strengthen future searches and attract even stronger talent over time.

Phase 4: Receiving Applications – Review Applications Purposefully

Every application represents someone who has invested time in your organization, making the candidate experience just as important as the evaluation process itself. Acknowledge every applicant, communicate clearly, and remember that each interaction shapes your reputation as an employer.

When reviewing applications, look beyond job titles and years of experience. Focus on evidence of results, patterns of growth, and the potential a candidate brings to the role. Apply consistent evaluation criteria, challenge unconscious bias, and avoid making assumptions about career paths or perceived gaps before seeking more information.

A thoughtful, respectful review process not only leads to stronger hiring decisions, it builds trust with current and future leaders alike.

Phase 5: Selecting – Structure and Consistency Matters

A strong interview process is just as important as attracting the right candidates. Build a diverse interview panel, ask consistent, role-related questions, and create an inclusive experience that allows every candidate to perform at their best.

Throughout the process, evaluate candidates against the criteria you established before the search began, rather than against one another. Look for specific examples of how they’ve led, solved challenges, and delivered results, while leaving space for meaningful questions and conversation.

Finally, don’t lose momentum. Clear communication and timely decisions create a positive candidate experience and help ensure top talent isn’t lost to another opportunity.

Phase 6: Offer & Onboarding – Finish the Search Well

The final stage of executive search is just as important as the beginning. Strong reference checks go beyond confirming employment; they should help validate leadership style, collaboration, decision-making, and how the candidate works with others in practice.

Once a decision is made, move with clarity and care. Keep communication timely, make the offer experience personal rather than transactional, and avoid surprises by ensuring expectations around role, compensation, and priorities have already been well established.

Onboarding begins before the offer is accepted. Thoughtful early engagement, clear preparation for day one, and intentional relationship-building help set the tone for long-term success. Leadership transitions start with how the offer and onboarding experience is handled.

Executive Search Is About More Than Filling a Role

The most successful executive searches create clarity around leadership expectations, engage stakeholders throughout the process, and help organizations identify leaders who align with their mission, culture, and long-term strategy.

When it’s done well, executive search becomes an investment in the future of the organization, rather than simply a hiring exercise.
